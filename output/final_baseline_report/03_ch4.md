# Chapter 4: Classification Results and Comparative Analysis

This chapter reports the results of all models on the 1,600 held-out test images (400 per class). It moves from training behaviour to the standalone deep models, the classical classifiers on raw pixels and on deep features, the six-predictor ensemble, per-class performance and finally the ROC and precision-recall analysis. All values were read from the result files written by the training notebook.

## 4.1 Training Behaviour

Table 6 summarises the last recorded epoch of each network, and Figures 7 and 8 show the full curves.

| Network | Epochs | Training accuracy | Validation accuracy | Training loss | Validation loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Custom CNN (256 px) | 20 (19 recorded) | 99.33% | 96.43% | 0.019 | 0.173 |
| InceptionV3 (256 px) | 10 | 94.29% | 92.50% | 0.170 | 0.213 |
| Xception (256 px) | 10 | 93.79% | 93.21% | 0.194 | 0.214 |
| CNN extractor (128 px) | 10 | 97.48% | 94.82% | 0.075 | 0.149 |
| InceptionV3 extractor (128 px) | 10 | 84.88% | 90.00% | 0.374 | 0.288 |
| Xception extractor (128 px) | 10 | 90.40% | 90.71% | 0.248 | 0.274 |

Table: Table 6. Accuracy and loss at the last recorded training epoch

![Figure 7. Training and validation accuracy and loss of the standalone deep models (256 px)](paper_assets/figures/fig_training_standalone.png){width=6.3in}

![Figure 8. Training and validation curves of the 128-pixel feature extractors](paper_assets/figures/fig_training_extractors.png){width=6.3in}

The custom CNN learnt the training data almost perfectly, reaching 99.33% training accuracy. Its validation loss reached its lowest value (0.137) at epoch 6 and then fluctuated upwards to 0.173, while training loss kept falling to 0.019. This widening gap is a clear sign of overfitting after the first third of training, which agrees with the Interim Report's observation that the CNN overfits after about ten epochs. Validation accuracy nevertheless stayed high (about 96%), so the overfitting reduced generalisation only moderately.

The pretrained networks behaved differently. Because only the new classification layers were trained (8,196 parameters for each standalone head), training and validation curves stayed close together and validation loss was still decreasing at epoch 10. This suggests that these models were not overfitting and might have improved slightly with more epochs. For the InceptionV3 extractor, validation accuracy (90.00%) exceeded training accuracy (84.88%). This is expected when dropout is active during training but switched off during validation.

## 4.2 Standalone Deep Models

| Model | Accuracy | Macro precision | Macro recall | Macro F1 | F1 glioma | F1 meningioma | F1 no tumour | F1 pituitary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Custom CNN | **90.44%** | **0.911** | **0.904** | **0.903** | **0.852** | **0.873** | 0.934 | **0.953** |
| Xception | 87.62% | 0.887 | 0.876 | 0.874 | 0.799 | 0.813 | **0.952** | 0.933 |
| InceptionV3 | 86.62% | 0.871 | 0.866 | 0.863 | 0.803 | 0.798 | 0.912 | 0.940 |

Table: Table 7. Test results of the standalone deep models (n = 1,600)

The custom CNN was the most accurate single model (90.44%), ahead of Xception (87.62%) and InceptionV3 (86.62%). It also had the highest macro F1 and the best F1 for three of the four classes. Xception was best only for no tumour. The ranking is the opposite of hypothesis H1, which expected the pretrained networks to win.

Two explanations are plausible. First, the pretrained backbones were frozen, so their features were learnt on natural photographs and were not adapted to MRI; only a single softmax layer on top was trained. Second, the custom CNN, although much smaller, adapted all of its weights to this dataset. Table 8 shows the cost side of the comparison.

| Model | Total parameters | Trainable parameters |
|---|---|---|
| Custom CNN | 3.31 million | 3.31 million |
| InceptionV3 (frozen backbone + softmax head) | 21.81 million | 8,196 |
| Xception (frozen backbone + softmax head) | 20.87 million | 8,196 |

Table: Table 8. Model size of the standalone deep models

The custom CNN is about six times smaller than either pretrained network and still more accurate, so it is also the most computationally efficient choice for inference. The pretrained networks were, however, cheap to train, because almost all of their weights were fixed.

All three models found glioma hardest. Glioma recall was 76.50% for the CNN, 70.75% for InceptionV3 and 68.75% for Xception, compared with 99.00% to 99.50% for no tumour.

## 4.3 Classical Machine Learning on Raw Pixels versus Deep Features

Table 9 compares the classical classifiers on raw pixels and on the 256 deep features of each extractor.

| Classifier | Raw pixels (65,536 features) | CNN features | InceptionV3 features | Xception features |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random Forest | 87.00% | **89.94%** | 86.75% | 88.38% |
| Support Vector Machine | 82.25% | 89.69% | 85.38% | 87.69% |
| Decision Tree | 77.88% | 86.50% | 83.69% | 86.00% |
| Logistic Regression | 79.87% | n/a | n/a | n/a |
| Soft vote (RF + DT + SVM) | 84.06% | 89.31% | 86.12% | 88.31% |
| Soft vote macro F1 | 0.837 | 0.892 | 0.859 | 0.881 |

Table: Table 9. Test accuracy of classical classifiers on raw pixels and on deep features (n = 1,600)

Deep features improved every classifier. The largest gains were for the SVM, from 82.25% on raw pixels to 89.69% on CNN features (+7.44 points), and the Decision Tree, from 77.88% to 86.50% (+8.62 points). The Random Forest was already strong on raw pixels (87.00%) and gained less (+2.94 points to 89.94%). On raw pixels the Random Forest was the best classical model, which shows that an ensemble of trees can cope with very high-dimensional input better than a single tree or a linear model.

The source of the features mattered. CNN features gave the best result for every classifier, Xception features came second and InceptionV3 features third. This repeats the ranking of the standalone networks and again contradicts H1. It also matches the extractor training curves: the InceptionV3 extractor had the lowest validation accuracy (90.00%).

Computational cost differed by orders of magnitude (Table 10). On raw pixels, the SVM needed 5,812.1 seconds (about 97 minutes) and Logistic Regression 505.3 seconds, whereas on 256 deep features every classical classifier trained in under 4 seconds.

| Classifier | Raw pixels | CNN features | InceptionV3 features | Xception features |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random Forest | 61.9 s | 3.0 s | 0.8 s | 0.8 s |
| Support Vector Machine | 5,812.1 s | 2.7 s | 3.8 s | 2.8 s |
| Decision Tree | 340.0 s | 1.3 s | 0.1 s | 0.2 s |
| Logistic Regression | 505.3 s | n/a | n/a | n/a |

Table: Table 10. Training time of the classical classifiers (Google Colab)

The soft vote of RF, DT and SVM did not always beat its best member. On CNN features it reached 89.31%, slightly below the Random Forest alone (89.94%), because the weaker Decision Tree pulls the average down. Combining models helps most when the members are similarly strong and make different errors (Dietterich, 2000).

## 4.4 Six-Predictor Majority and Confidence-Weighted Ensemble

### 4.4.1 Overall Results

| Ensemble | Accuracy | Macro precision | Macro recall | Macro F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Six-predictor majority vote** | **92.50%** | **0.930** | **0.925** | **0.924** |
| Six-predictor confidence-weighted vote | 92.25% | 0.928 | 0.922 | 0.921 |
| Best single model (custom CNN) | 90.44% | 0.911 | 0.904 | 0.903 |
| Best classical model (RF on CNN features) | 89.94% | 0.903 | 0.899 | 0.898 |

Table: Table 11. Test results of the six-predictor ensembles

The majority vote reached 92.50% accuracy, 2.06 points above the best single model and 2.56 points above the best classical model. Its macro F1 (0.924) was also the highest of all configurations. Hypothesis H2 is therefore supported. The confidence-weighted vote reached 92.25%, so weighting by confidence did not improve accuracy here: the two rules disagree on only 30 of the 1,600 images, and on those the majority vote was right 13 times and the confidence-weighted vote 9 times (the remaining 8 were wrong under both rules). Confidence weighting still has a practical advantage, because it produces a graded confidence score for every image, which the web application uses. Figure 9 places all 22 configurations on one chart.

![Figure 9. Test accuracy of all 22 configurations](paper_assets/figures_final/fig_accuracy_bars.png){width=5.6in}

Inside the ensemble pipeline, test images are loaded with OpenCV instead of the Keras image loader. The two resize routines differ slightly, so the three standalone members score 90.50% (CNN), 86.38% (InceptionV3) and 87.75% (Xception) in the ensemble, close to but not identical with Table 7.

### 4.4.2 How the Ensemble Builds Up

Table 12 adds the predictors one at a time, summing their probability vectors.

| Predictors combined | Accuracy |
|---|---|
| CNN | 90.50% |
| + InceptionV3 | 91.38% |
| + Xception | 91.12% |
| + CNN features with classical soft vote | 91.94% |
| + InceptionV3 features with classical soft vote | 92.38% |
| + Xception features with classical soft vote (all six) | 92.25% |

Table: Table 12. Accuracy as predictors are added (confidence-weighted sum)

Most of the gain came from the first two additions and from the classical predictors on deep features. The last predictor did not add accuracy. The value of a six-member ensemble therefore lies less in its average accuracy than in the reliability information provided by agreement between the members, as the next section shows.

### 4.4.3 Agreement Between the Six Predictors

| Votes for the winning class | Images | Share of test set | Majority-vote accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 of 6 (unanimous) | 1,153 | 72.06% | 98.44% |
| 5 of 6 | 211 | 13.19% | 93.36% |
| 4 of 6 | 155 | 9.69% | 67.10% |
| 3 of 6 | 74 | 4.63% | 52.70% |
| 2 of 6 | 7 | 0.44% | 71.43% |

Table: Table 13. Accuracy of the majority vote by number of agreeing predictors

When all six predictors agreed (1,153 images, 72% of the test set), the majority vote was correct 98.44% of the time. When the winning class received only three votes (74 images), accuracy fell to 52.70%, close to a coin toss. The number of agreeing votes is therefore a strong, easily explained indicator of reliability. For 52 images (3.25%) all six predictors were wrong, so no voting rule could have rescued them; these set a practical ceiling of about 96.75% for any combination of these six members.

No tumour and pituitary were classified almost perfectly. Every no-tumour image was recognised (100% recall), and pituitary reached 97% precision and recall.

Glioma was the clear weak point, with 77.50% recall. Of the 90 missed gliomas, 65 (16.25% of all gliomas) were predicted as meningioma, 23 (5.75%) as no tumour and 2 as pituitary. The glioma-to-meningioma confusion also explains meningioma's lower precision (83.59%): most of the images wrongly labelled meningioma were gliomas. When the model predicted glioma, however, it was almost always right (97.48% precision), so its typical failure is to miss a glioma rather than to raise a false alarm.

Clinically, the 23 gliomas predicted as "no tumour" are the most serious errors, because a missed tumour could delay treatment. The ensemble roughly halved this error compared with the custom CNN alone, which predicted 40 gliomas as no tumour. The same pattern holds for every configuration in Figure 13: glioma always has the lowest F1, and no tumour and pituitary the highest. Glioma is the most heterogeneous class. Its lesions vary in size, shape and contrast, and well-defined gliomas can resemble meningiomas on a single slice.

## 4.6 ROC and Precision-Recall Analysis

Figure 14 shows one-vs-rest ROC and precision-recall curves for the three deep networks, and Table 16 gives the areas under them.

![Figure 14. One-vs-rest ROC (top) and precision-recall (bottom) curves of the standalone deep models](paper_assets/figures/fig_roc_pr.png){width=6.3in}

| Model | Glioma | Meningioma | No tumour | Pituitary | Macro ROC AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Custom CNN | 0.8995 / 0.883 | 0.9786 / 0.936 | 0.9941 / 0.974 | 0.9972 / 0.988 | 0.9673 |
| InceptionV3 | 0.9356 / 0.893 | 0.9529 / 0.852 | 0.9974 / 0.992 | 0.9926 / 0.977 | **0.9696** |
| Xception | 0.9152 / 0.884 | 0.9545 / 0.865 | 0.9973 / 0.991 | 0.9922 / 0.979 | 0.9648 |

Table: Table 16. ROC AUC / average precision per class (one-vs-rest)

All three networks separated the classes well (macro ROC AUC 0.965 to 0.970), and no tumour and pituitary had AUCs above 0.99. Glioma had the lowest AUC for every model, consistent with its low recall.

An interesting difference appears between accuracy and ROC AUC. InceptionV3 had the lowest accuracy (86.62%) but the highest macro ROC AUC (0.9696) and the best glioma AUC (0.9356). The custom CNN had the best accuracy but the lowest glioma AUC (0.8995). Accuracy only checks whether the top class is right. ROC AUC instead measures how well the predicted probabilities rank positive images above negative ones across all thresholds. InceptionV3's probabilities therefore carry useful ranking information that is lost when only the top class is taken, which helps explain why its predictions still add value inside the ensemble (Table 12). The precision-recall curves show the same picture: the CNN's glioma precision drops sharply once recall exceeds about 80%, whereas meningioma is the hardest class for the two pretrained networks (average precision 0.852 and 0.865).

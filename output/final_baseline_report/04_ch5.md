# Chapter 5: Explainable AI (Grad-CAM and EAA-IoU) and the Clinical Web Application

This chapter examines what the three deep networks look at when they make a decision. It first inspects Grad-CAM heatmaps for correct and misclassified images, then evaluates the EAA-IoU agreement score on all 1,600 test images, calibrates an escalation threshold and finally walks through the Streamlit prototype.

## 5.1 Visual Grad-CAM Analysis

For each class, two test images were selected: the correctly classified image with the highest ensemble confidence, and a confidently misclassified image from the class's most frequent error. No-tumour images were never misclassified, so the no-tumour error example is the most frequent error into that class, a glioma predicted as no tumour. In Figures 15 and 16, red marks regions that raised the score of the predicted class most and blue marks regions with little influence.

### 5.1.1 Correctly Classified Images

![Figure 15. Grad-CAM of the custom CNN, InceptionV3 and Xception for the most confident correct prediction in each class](paper_assets/figures_final/fig_gradcam_correct.png){width=6.0in}

Three patterns are visible in Figure 15:

- **The pretrained networks produce broad, smooth maps that usually cover the lesion.** For the meningioma, both InceptionV3 and Xception centre their attention on the large, sharply bounded mass. For the pituitary tumour, Xception concentrates on the sellar region where the pituitary gland lies, which is anatomically appropriate. For the glioma, InceptionV3 covers the lesion and the surrounding hemisphere, while Xception peaks slightly to one side of it.
- **The custom CNN produces small, scattered hotspots.** Its maps highlight a few small regions, and some of them lie outside the brain. For the glioma, its strongest hotspot sits on the skull at the top of the image, and for the meningioma its hotspots lie along the image borders rather than on the mass. This means that the most accurate single model does not necessarily base its decision on the lesion, a reminder that accuracy and plausibility are different properties (Rudin, 2019).
- **For no tumour, attention is spread over the whole brain.** With no lesion to localise, the pretrained networks attend broadly to the central brain, and the CNN to scattered points including the ventricles. This is expected: the decision "no tumour" depends on the absence of a finding everywhere.

### 5.1.2 Misclassified Images

![Figure 16. Grad-CAM for confidently misclassified images (the most frequent error of each class)](paper_assets/figures_final/fig_gradcam_errors.png){width=6.0in}

The errors in Figure 16 fall into two different types, which matters for how they can be detected:

- **Agreement on the lesion, wrong interpretation.** In the most common error, a glioma predicted as meningioma (65 cases), InceptionV3 and Xception both focus on the large, bright, well-defined lesion, and even the CNN's hotspots lie on it. The networks look at the right place but read the lesion's appearance as typical of meningioma. Because the models agree, an agreement-based check cannot catch this type of error.
- **Attention in the wrong place.** In the most dangerous error, a glioma predicted as no tumour with 99.6% confidence, both pretrained networks focus on the opposite, normal-looking hemisphere and the midline rather than on the lesion. The meningioma predicted as pituitary and the pituitary predicted as meningioma (both sagittal slices) show the networks attending to different regions: in the first case InceptionV3 looks at the brainstem and neck while Xception looks at the upper brain, and in the second both networks highlight a horizontal band at the level of the orbits and anterior skull base rather than the sellar region. These are the errors that disagreement between the maps can reveal.

These examples motivate the quantitative analysis: if many errors involve attention in the wrong place, and if the networks attend to different places when they are wrong, low agreement between their maps should signal an unreliable prediction.

## 5.2 Quantitative EAA-IoU Evaluation

### 5.2.1 A Worked Example

Figure 17 and Table 17 show how EAA-IoU is computed for three real test images. In each image, the three Grad-CAM maps are binarised at 0.5, the overlap of every pair is measured, and the three pair IoUs are averaged.

![Figure 17. EAA-IoU step by step for three test images. For each image, top row: input and Grad-CAM of each network with the 0.5 mask outlined in white; bottom row: overlap of each pair of masks and the resulting IoU](paper_assets/figures_final/fig_eaa_example_baseline.png){width=5.6in}

| Quantity | A. High agreement | B. Moderate agreement | C. Low agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| True class / ensemble prediction | Glioma / Glioma (correct) | Pituitary / Pituitary (correct) | Glioma / Meningioma (wrong) |
| Ensemble confidence | 92.04% | 99.14% | 40.05% |
| Mask size: CNN / InceptionV3 / Xception (% of image) | 1.0 / 29.1 / 34.7 | 0.3 / 29.9 / 32.5 | 0.3 / 2.2 / 8.9 |
| CNN vs Xception: intersection / union = IoU | 570 / 22,798 = 0.025 | 111 / 21,360 = 0.005 | 0 / 6,027 = 0.000 |
| CNN vs InceptionV3 | 528 / 19,201 = 0.028 | 165 / 19,616 = 0.008 | 0 / 1,659 = 0.000 |
| Xception vs InceptionV3 | 18,678 / 23,097 = 0.809 | 12,251 / 28,587 = 0.429 | 0 / 7,280 = 0.000 |
| **EAA-IoU (mean of the three)** | **0.287** | **0.147** | **0.000** |
| Agreement band and action | Strong (0.20 or above) | Moderate (0.10 to 0.20) | Below 0.10: escalate |

Table: Table 17. The numbers behind the EAA-IoU worked example

Image A has the highest EAA-IoU of all 1,600 test images. Even there, the CNN's mask covers only about 1% of the image, so its pairs score close to zero, and the high score comes almost entirely from the two pretrained networks agreeing (IoU 0.809). In image C, the ensemble was wrong and only 40.05% confident, the three masks do not overlap at all, and EAA-IoU is zero.

### 5.2.2 Agreement on the Whole Test Set

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean EAA-IoU, all 1,600 images | 0.0985 |
| Mean EAA-IoU, correctly classified images (1,476) | 0.1029 |
| Mean EAA-IoU, misclassified images (124) | 0.0471 |
| Median EAA-IoU, correct / misclassified | 0.1043 / 0.0237 |
| Images with EAA-IoU of exactly 0 | 2.62% |
| AUROC of low EAA-IoU as an error detector | 0.7525 |
| AUROC of low ensemble confidence as an error detector | 0.9127 |

Table: Table 18. EAA-IoU on the 1,600 test images (errors counted for the confidence-weighted prediction, which the maps explain)

![Figure 18. EAA-IoU on all 1,600 test images: by true class, correct vs misclassified, and as an error detector](paper_assets/figures/fig_eaa_iou.png){width=6.3in}

Correctly classified images had about twice the mean agreement of misclassified images (0.1029 against 0.0471), and the median for misclassified images (0.0237) was less than a quarter of that for correct images (0.1043). Used as an error detector, low EAA-IoU reached an AUROC of 0.7525, well above the 0.5 expected by chance. This supports hypothesis H3: when the three networks attend to different regions, the ensemble is more likely to be wrong.

The ensemble's own confidence was, however, a stronger error signal (AUROC 0.9127). EAA-IoU is therefore a genuine but secondary uncertainty signal. Its value lies in being visual and independent of the probability outputs: it tells the clinician not only that the system is unsure, but also that the networks are looking at different parts of the image.

### 5.2.3 Which Pairs Drive the Score

| Pair | Mean IoU (all 1,600 images) |
|---|---|
| Xception vs InceptionV3 | 0.285 |
| CNN vs Xception | 0.0057 |
| CNN vs InceptionV3 | 0.0049 |

Table: Table 19. Mean pairwise IoU between the Grad-CAM masks

The score is driven almost entirely by the two pretrained networks. Pairs involving the custom CNN average about 0.005, because the CNN's maps are sharp and highly localised (in the worked examples its 0.5 mask covers 1% of the image or less), whereas the pretrained networks' maps are broad and smooth (masks of about 30% of the image in the same examples). Two thresholded maps of such different sizes can hardly overlap much, even when the small one lies inside the large one. The difference reflects the architectures: the CNN's last convolutional layer has a finer spatial grid and feeds a Flatten and Dense head, while InceptionV3 and Xception end in coarse feature maps and global average pooling. EAA-IoU therefore mainly measures whether InceptionV3 and Xception agree, with the CNN contributing little.

### 5.2.4 Agreement Across Classes

| Class | Mean EAA-IoU | Xception vs InceptionV3 IoU | Ensemble recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| No tumour | **0.1453** | 0.424 | 100.00% |
| Glioma | 0.0982 | 0.282 | 77.50% |
| Pituitary | 0.0823 | 0.240 | 97.00% |
| Meningioma | 0.0683 | 0.195 | 95.50% |

Table: Table 20. Attention agreement and recall by true class

No tumour had the highest agreement, consistent with its perfect recall: with no lesion, both pretrained networks spread attention over the central brain, and broad maps overlap easily. Contrary to the expectation stated during the Interim stage, glioma did not have the lowest agreement; meningioma and pituitary did. Glioma's low recall is therefore not explained by disagreement alone. This matches the visual analysis in Section 5.1.2: in the most frequent glioma error, the networks agree on the lesion but misread it as meningioma. Agreement-based checks can detect errors caused by attention in the wrong place, but not errors in which all networks make the same misjudgement.

## 5.3 Threshold Calibration for Clinical Escalation

The Interim Report proposed escalating images with EAA-IoU below 0.40 and treating 0.65 or more as strong agreement. These levels were set before any results existed. On the test set the highest EAA-IoU was 0.287, so a 0.40 threshold would flag every image and give no useful information. Thresholds were therefore recalibrated from the observed distribution (Tables 21 and 22).

| Threshold (flag if EAA-IoU below) | Images flagged | Errors caught | Error rate in flagged images | Error rate in unflagged images |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 28.62% | 61.29% | 16.59% | 4.20% |
| **0.10 (adopted)** | **50.81%** | **82.26%** | **12.55%** | **2.80%** |
| 0.20 | 94.19% | 99.19% | 8.16% | 1.08% |
| 0.40 (Interim proposal) | 100.00% | 100.00% | 7.75% | n/a |

Table: Table 21. Effect of the escalation threshold (1,600 test images, 124 errors)

| EAA-IoU band | Images | Share of test set | Errors | Error rate in band | Share of all errors |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Below 0.10 (alert) | 813 | 50.81% | 102 | 12.55% | 82.26% |
| 0.10 to 0.20 (moderate) | 694 | 43.38% | 21 | 3.03% | 16.94% |
| 0.20 or above (strong) | 93 | 5.81% | 1 | 1.08% | 0.81% |

Table: Table 22. Test images and errors by agreement band

A threshold of 0.10 was adopted for the alert, and 0.20 as the level of strong agreement. At 0.10, about half of the images are flagged and 82.26% of all errors are among them; the error rate in the unflagged half falls to 2.80%, compared with 7.75% overall. In the strong-agreement band, only one of 93 images was misclassified. For a decision-support tool this is a sensible trade-off: the radiologist reviews every image anyway, and the alert tells them which half deserves the closest attention.

The limitation must be stated plainly. Flagging half of all images is a broad filter, and ensemble confidence does better at the same flag rate: flagging the 813 least confident images would catch 95.97% of errors. EAA-IoU should therefore complement, not replace, the confidence score. The Streamlit application shows both.

## 5.4 Streamlit Prototype Walkthrough

The prototype brings the classifier and the explanations together in one page. Figures 19 and 20 are screenshots of the Live Diagnosis page running the trained models on two test images.

![Figure 19. Live Diagnosis page for a glioma correctly classified with strong attention agreement](paper_assets/figures_final/fig_app_high.png){width=4.6in}

**Example 1: strong agreement (Figure 19).** A coronal glioma slice is uploaded. The six predictors all vote for glioma, and the confidence-weighted ensemble score is 91.7%. The class probability chart shows almost all probability on glioma. The Grad-CAM row shows that Xception and InceptionV3 both highlight the upper part of the brain around the lesion (pair IoU 0.790), the CNN's map is sparse, and the consensus map combines the three. The EAA-IoU of 0.283 lies above 0.20, so the page reports strong agreement. The per-model vote breakdown shows each predictor's vote and probability.

![Figure 20. Live Diagnosis page for a glioma misclassified as meningioma, with the escalation alert](paper_assets/figures_final/fig_app_alert.png){width=4.6in}

**Example 2: escalation (Figure 20).** An axial glioma slice is uploaded. The ensemble predicts meningioma with a score of only 45.7%, and the votes are split: three predictors vote meningioma, two vote no tumour and one votes glioma. The three heatmaps highlight different regions, the pair IoUs are all close to zero, and EAA-IoU is 0.004. The page therefore shows a red alert: *"Escalate for radiologist review: the three networks look at different regions."* This is exactly the behaviour intended: a wrong prediction is not hidden behind a confident label but is explicitly marked as unreliable.

The application loads all six predictors once at start-up and caches them, so later uploads do not reload the models. Because it reads images with the Python Imaging Library rather than OpenCV, its scores can differ slightly from the offline evaluation (for example, EAA-IoU 0.283 against 0.287 for the first image). Every result page carries the statement that the tool is for research and does not replace a qualified radiologist. The other pages of the application present the research results interactively: model performance, confusion matrices, the EAA-IoU method and the model comparison.
